## The through-line (every section serves this)

A dark standard siren does not know its redshift; a host catalog supplies
it. This paper's claim: when two catalogs trace the same large-scale
structure with different number density and different clustering bias, the
fraction of mergers hosted by each is a parameter of the same likelihood
that measures the expansion rate, and both are measured jointly from the
same events. The story, in order:

1. One catalog at a time fails, asymmetrically: the dense catalog absorbs
   foreign events at a price in width; the sparse catalog rails.
2. The mixture repairs the misassignment and measures both parameters; the
   second, hundred-times-sparser catalog SHARPENS H0 by a factor 2.9 rather
   than diluting it.
3. The gain has a per-event origin: on events it actually hosts, at equal
   event count, the sparse catalog is a factor 5.1 sharper, because each
   line of sight holds a handful of candidate hosts rather than a smooth
   crowd. (This is the promoted analysis 0.)
4. Realism: flux-limited catalogs preserve the measurement provided the
   completeness follows from the survey's magnitude limit and the catalog's
   luminosity function, and provided the AGN number density is known. The
   density is the one input that matters: f_AGN follows it at 0.45 per dex;
   relative survey depth, by contrast, moves nothing (the analysis-6 null);
   H0 is immune above ~80% completeness and density-sensitive at ~10%.
   With the densities free, the data themselves prefer luminosity-function
   completeness over number-count completeness by 12 to 18 e-folds and
   recover the input galaxy density across three realisations.
5. Validation: the whole construction repeats across five realisations of
   the simulated universe; a sky-scramble null shows host association, not
   catalog structure, carries f_AGN; endpoint identities; the carried MC
   error.
6. Discussion: what a real analysis needs (AGN density, survey-derived
   completeness, the untested population freedom) and who the number is
   for.

The reader should finish each section holding one claim, and the next
section's opening should need it.
