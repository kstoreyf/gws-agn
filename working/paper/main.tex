\begin{abstract}
We measure the Hubble constant \Hz\ and the fraction \fagn\ of compact-binary
mergers hosted by active galactic nuclei (AGN) jointly, from \EvNobs\ simulated
dark standard sirens and two host catalogs, galaxies and AGN, tracing one
large-scale structure. Their line-of-sight redshift priors enter the
likelihood as a mixture weighted by \fagn. With the source population at
its input values and both catalogs complete, we find
$\Hz = \HzeroJoint$~\kmsmpc\ and $\fagn = \FagnJoint$: the realised hosted
fraction (\FagnTruthReal) lies inside the 68\% interval, the input expansion
rate inside the 90\%, and the \Hz\ interval is a factor of
\HzeroWidthRatio\ narrower than the galaxy catalog's alone. Both parameters'
mean offsets across five realisations are consistent with zero, and with
\Hz\ held at its input value, scrambling the events' sky positions collapses
\fagn\ from \FagnRecord\ to \FagnNull: host association carries the
fraction. Flux-limited catalogs preserve the measurement on one realisation
when completeness follows from each catalog's magnitude limit and luminosity
function: with the host densities free the fraction is only bounded, but the
recovered galaxy density lies inside the 68\%
interval in three realisations, while completeness estimated from observed
counts overstates it by up to \PixelAnchorBiasMaxDex~dex, disfavoured by
\SeedLnBFmin\ to \SeedLnBFmax\ $e$-folds. A factor-of-two error in the assumed AGN density moves \Hz\ by
\HzDensityShiftFactorTwoDeep~\kmsmpc\ above \CompMTwenty\%
completeness, by \HzDensityShiftFactorTwo\ at \CompMEighteen\%. The AGN-hosted fraction is thus a branching ratio
measurable from the events that measure the expansion rate, provided the AGN
density is known.
\end{abstract}
