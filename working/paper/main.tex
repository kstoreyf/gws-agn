\title{A dark standard siren measurement of the Hubble constant and the
AGN-hosted fraction of compact-binary mergers}

\author{I.~Maga\~na Hernandez}
\affiliation{Department of Physics, Carnegie Mellon University, Pittsburgh, PA
15213, USA}

\begin{abstract}
We measure the Hubble constant \Hz\ and the fraction \fagn\ of compact-binary
mergers hosted by active galactic nuclei (AGN) jointly, from \EvNobs\ simulated
dark standard sirens and two host catalogs (galaxies and AGN) that trace
the same simulated large-scale structure with a number-density contrast of
\DensityRatio\ and a clustering-bias contrast of \BiasRatioMeas. Each catalog
supplies a line-of-sight redshift prior, the two priors enter the likelihood as
a mixture with weight \fagn, and the fraction is identified by which catalog's
sky and redshift structure the events prefer. With the source population held at
its input values and both catalogs complete, we find
$\Hz = \HzeroJoint$~\kmsmpc\ and $\fagn = \FagnJoint$: the realised hosted
fraction (\FagnTruthReal, from \EvNhostAgn\ of the \EvNobs\ events hosted by
AGN) lies inside the 68\% interval, and the input expansion rate
(\HzeroTruth~\kmsmpc) inside the 90\%. The same events analysed against the galaxy catalog alone
give $\Hz = \HzeroGal$~\kmsmpc: freeing the mixture and adding a catalog one
hundred times sparser sharpens \Hz\ by a factor of \HzeroWidthRatio\ rather
than diluting it. Scrambling the event sky positions, with \Hz\ held fixed, collapses the
recovered fraction from \FagnRecord\ to \FagnNull, so the fraction is measured from host
association rather than from the catalogs' redshift envelopes. Across five
independent realisations of the simulated universe the joint fit recovers both
parameters, with mean offsets of $\ClosureHzero$~\kmsmpc\ and
$\ClosureFagnReal$. The branching ratio between AGN-assisted and field mergers
is therefore measurable from the same events that measure the expansion rate,
at no cost to it, provided the AGN number density is known.
\end{abstract}
