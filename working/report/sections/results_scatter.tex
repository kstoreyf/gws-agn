\section{Catalog realisation and the two-tracer measurement}
\label{sec:scatter}

The two-tracer numbers of \S\ref{sec:selection} and \S\ref{sec:completeness:two}
come from a single draw of the host field, and \S\ref{sec:budget:samplevariance}
shows that for a single tracer the realisation term is comparable to the
statistical one. Whether the multitracer combination cancels part of it is a
different question, and an interesting one: the two tracers sample the same
underlying density field, so a realisation that happens to over-populate a
structure over-populates it in both, and the \emph{contrast} the fraction is
measured from may be more stable than either prior separately.

We measure this by rebuilding the matched two-tracer mock --- host field, tracer
subset, events, posterior samples and targeted injection campaign --- over
\SdsN\ independent realisations and repeating the fraction scan and the joint
scan on each. These realisations were generated before the sky-width repair of
\S\ref{sec:budget:oracle}, so their absolute expansion-rate offsets carry that
known defect in full; the realisation-to-realisation \emph{scatter}, and its
ratio to the quoted widths, is the estimand here, and the comparison between
the fraction and the distance scale is internal to the ensemble.

The answer is that the contrast is realisation-stable and the distance scale
is not. At fixed true expansion rate, the fraction's mean offset over
realisations is \SdsFscanOffset$\pm$\SdsFscanSem\ (\SdsFscanSig$\sigma$ from
zero) with a scatter of \SdsFscanScatter\ against a mean quoted 68\%
half-width of \SdsFscanHw: a realisation factor of \SdsFscanFactor, i.e.\ the
quoted intervals for \fagn\ are honest about the catalog draw. On the joint
grid the same statement holds --- offset \SdsFOffset$\pm$\SdsFSem, scatter
\SdsFScatter\ against \SdsFHw, factor \SdsFFactor. The expansion rate is the
opposite: a mean offset of \SdsHzeroOffset$\pm$\SdsHzeroSem~\kmsmpc\ ---
dominated by the pre-repair generator defect --- with a scatter of
\SdsHzeroScatter~\kmsmpc\ against a mean quoted half-width of \SdsHzeroHw, a
factor of \SdsHzeroFactor. The single-tracer factor of \CloSeedUnderFactor\
(\S\ref{sec:budget:samplevariance}) is therefore not softened by the second
tracer for the distance scale, but the parameter the second tracer exists to
measure is realisation-stable at the quoted precision: the shared density
field drops out of the contrast, as the opening paragraph hoped, while \Hz\
keeps the full realisation term of whichever priors it leans on.

The \DeepFTgtSig$\sigma$-low pre-repair fraction of \S\ref{sec:selection} is
consistent with this ensemble: at scatter-over-width near unity, single-draw
offsets of that size occur with ordinary probability. The ensemble does show a small
mean deficit --- \SdsFOffset$\pm$\SdsFSem\ (\SdsFSig$\sigma$) on the joint
grid and \SdsFscanOffset$\pm$\SdsFscanSem\ (\SdsFscanSig$\sigma$) at fixed
\Hz\ --- which demands re-examination on a repaired ensemble, since at
expansion-rate offsets this large the distance scale and the fraction are not
fully decoupled. That ensemble follows.

Regenerating the same \SdsFixN\ realisations with the repaired generator of
\S\ref{sec:budget:oracle} settles both points. The expansion rate closes: the
post-repair ensemble recovers \Hz\ at
\SdsFixHzeroOffset$\pm$\SdsFixHzeroSem~\kmsmpc\ (\SdsFixHzeroSig$\sigma$ from
zero), so the two-tracer joint measurement is unbiased once the mock is a
genuine draw from the model the likelihood assumes, and what remains of the
realisation term --- scatter \SdsFixHzeroScatter~\kmsmpc\ against a quoted
half-width of \SdsFixHzeroHw, a factor of \SdsFixHzeroFactor\ --- is the
honest catalog-draw contribution rather than a defect. The fraction's small
deficit survives at reduced size --- \SdsFixFOffset$\pm$\SdsFixFSem\
(\SdsFixFSig$\sigma$) on the joint grid, with realisation factor
\SdsFixFFactor\ --- which places it at the same scale as the residual
estimator terms of \S\ref{sec:budget:oracle} rather than at the
distance-coupled size it had before the repair. The fraction remains
realisation-stable throughout; the distance scale becomes accurate as well as
stable.
